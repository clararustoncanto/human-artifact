01 / What matters now

A changing job is still a job.

Two different kinds of evidence help tell the story: a study of which jobs could be affected by generative AI, and employers’ forecasts for how skills may change.

03 / How work changes

Think in tasks, not job titles.

A job contains many tasks. AI might help with one task, while people remain responsible for context, accuracy, and decisions.

01

Draft or sort

AI can produce a first draft, summarize material, or organize information.

02

Check and interpret

A person verifies claims, finds missing context, and questions biased output.

03

Decide and own it

People weigh tradeoffs, communicate with others, and take responsibility for the result.

These are illustrative examples of a workflow, not measured shares of tasks. The ILO’s 2025 update emphasizes transformation of tasks and continued human input.

05 / Big takeaway
The future of work is shaped by the choices we make about AI.

Learning to use new tools matters. So do asking good questions, checking their answers, and deciding when a human should lead. The technology can change a task; people and institutions shape what that change means for workers.
